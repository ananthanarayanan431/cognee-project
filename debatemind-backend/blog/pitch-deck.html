The Problem

Every AI debate partner
forgets you the moment you leave.

Blind to patterns

It never notices the same logical fallacy you fall into every single time.

No sense of you

It can't tell you crumble on economics but dominate on ethics.

Doesn't compound

Generic practice that never gets sharper — so you never actually improve.

To get better at arguing, you need an opponent that pushes your specific weaknesses. That requires memory.

The Solution

An adaptive opponent that learns how you argue.

  • Pick a topic — or let the AI generate one — and debate by text or voice.
  • Every argument is graded and written into a persistent cognitive fingerprint.
  • Before each reply, the opponent recalls your weaknesses and aims straight at them.
  • The more you play, the harder it gets — in exactly your weak areas.
How the Memory Works

Remember → Recall → Improve → Forget

Master a weakness and it's deleted from the graph — the opponent stops wasting rounds on a fight you've already won.

Architecture

One memory. Two bots. Everything heavy, async.

The Novel Bit

One memory, two bots.

The text bot and the voice bot read and write the same fingerprint dataset.

Cross-modal recall

Reveal a weakness by voice…

…and it surfaces the next time you debate by text. And the reverse.

Hard to fake

A shared cognitive graph

Guided by a hand-authored OWL ontology of how people reason — the core moat.
